# Introduction

Oceans are one of Earth's greatest natural resources, covering over two-thirds of the planet's surface and supporting millions of species. They play a vital role in supplying food, providing raw materials, generating renewable energy, transporting goods, supporting tourism, and even supplying freshwater through desalination. As the world's population continues to grow, the demand for marine resources is increasing rapidly. This makes the sustainable management of oceans and fisheries essential for protecting marine ecosystems while meeting human needs.

In this chapter, you will learn about the importance of oceans as a resource, the factors that make certain regions highly productive for fishing, the causes and impacts of overfishing, and the strategies used by governments and international organizations to ensure the sustainable management of marine resources.

# Section 1: The Oceans as a Resource

## 🌍 Distribution of Oceans

Approximately **71% of Earth's surface is covered by oceans**, making them the planet's largest ecosystem. However, only about **10% of the ocean consists of continental shelves**, yet these shallow coastal waters provide **around 95% of the world's commercial fish catch**.

The oceans are among Earth's most valuable natural resources because they provide food, raw materials, renewable energy, transport routes, tourism opportunities, and freshwater through desalination.

---

## 🍽️ Food Resources

The oceans provide an important source of food for billions of people worldwide.

Marine food resources include:

- **Finfish** (e.g. tuna, cod, sardines)
- **Shellfish** (e.g. prawns, crabs, oysters, mussels)
- **Seaweeds and other aquatic plants**

Most commercial fishing takes place on **continental shelves** because these shallow waters receive abundant sunlight and contain high levels of dissolved oxygen. These conditions encourage the growth of **phytoplankton** and **zooplankton**, forming the base of the marine food chain and creating ideal breeding grounds for fish.

---

## 🪨 Chemicals and Building Materials

Oceans contain many valuable dissolved substances and mineral resources.

Examples include:

- Salt
- Gold
- Tin
- Diamonds
- Sand
- Gravel
- Oil

Salt is commonly produced by evaporating seawater in **salt lagoons**. Minerals can be extracted through seabed mining, while **offshore oil rigs** drill beneath the ocean floor to obtain petroleum.

---

## ⚡ Wave and Tidal Energy

The movement of seawater provides a valuable source of renewable energy.

### Wave Energy

Wave power systems use floating platforms connected by hinges. As waves move the platform, mechanical energy is converted into electrical energy.

### Tidal Energy

Tidal power stations use underwater turbines that rotate as tides rise and fall. Since tides occur at predictable times every day, tidal energy is generally considered more reliable than wave energy.

---

## 🚢 Transport

Oceans provide the world's most important transport routes for international trade.

Advantages include:

- Low-cost transportation of goods
- Ability to carry very large cargoes
- Efficient international trade
- Lower transportation costs than air transport

Today, the majority of global trade depends on ocean shipping.

---

## 🏖️ Tourism

Coastal regions attract millions of visitors every year, contributing significantly to local economies.

Popular marine tourism activities include:

- Swimming
- Snorkeling
- Scuba diving
- Windsurfing
- Jet skiing
- Recreational fishing
- Sunbathing

Tourism supports employment and businesses such as hotels, restaurants, transport services, and recreational facilities.

---

## 💧 Potential for Safe Drinking Water

Countries with limited freshwater resources can obtain drinking water through **desalination**.

Desalination removes dissolved salts from seawater, producing **potable (safe drinking) water**. This technology is especially important in arid countries where freshwater supplies are scarce.

---

### 📌 Key Takeaways

- Oceans cover **71%** of Earth's surface.
- Only **10%** of the ocean is continental shelf, yet it provides **95%** of the world's commercial fish catch.
- Oceans provide food, minerals, energy, transport routes, tourism opportunities, and freshwater.
- Continental shelves are highly productive because they receive abundant sunlight and dissolved oxygen.
- Desalination converts seawater into safe drinking water for water-scarce regions.
