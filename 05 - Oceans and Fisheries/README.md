🌍 **Distribution of Oceans**

Approximately **71% of Earth's surface is covered by oceans**, making them the largest ecosystem on Earth. However, only about **10% of the ocean consists of continental shelves**, yet these shallow coastal waters provide **around 95% of the world's commercial fish catch**.

The oceans are one of Earth's most valuable natural resources. They provide food, raw materials, renewable energy, transport routes, tourism opportunities, and even freshwater through desalination.

---

🍽️ **Food Resources**

The oceans provide an important source of food for billions of people worldwide.

Marine food resources include:

- **Finfish** (e.g. tuna, cod, sardines)
- **Shellfish** (e.g. prawns, crabs, oysters and mussels)
- **Seaweeds and other aquatic plants**

The world's major fisheries are located on **continental shelves** because these shallow waters:

- Receive abundant sunlight.
- Contain high levels of dissolved oxygen.
- Support the growth of **phytoplankton** and **zooplankton**.
- Provide ideal breeding and feeding grounds for fish.

---

🪨 **Chemicals and Building Materials**

The oceans contain many valuable dissolved substances and mineral resources.

Examples include:

- Salt
- Gold
- Tin
- Diamonds
- Sand
- Gravel
- Oil

Salt is commonly produced by evaporating seawater in **salt lagoons**. Minerals can be extracted from the seabed, while **offshore oil rigs** drill beneath the ocean floor to obtain petroleum.

---

⚡ **Wave and Tidal Energy**

The movement of seawater provides an important source of renewable energy.

**Wave Energy**

Wave power systems use floating platforms connected by hinges. As waves move the platform, mechanical energy is converted into electrical energy.

**Tidal Energy**

Tidal power stations use underwater turbines that rotate as tides rise and fall. Since tides occur at predictable times every day, tidal energy is generally more reliable than wave energy.

---

🚢 **Transport**

Oceans provide the world's most important transport routes for international trade.

Advantages include:

- Low-cost transportation of goods.
- Ability to carry very large cargoes.
- Efficient international trade.
- Lower transport costs than air freight.

Today, the majority of global trade depends on ocean shipping.

---

🏖️ **Tourism**

Coastal regions attract millions of visitors every year and contribute significantly to local economies.

Popular marine tourism activities include:

- Swimming
- Snorkelling
- Scuba diving
- Windsurfing
- Jet skiing
- Recreational fishing
- Sunbathing

Marine tourism supports employment and businesses such as hotels, restaurants, transport services, and recreational facilities.

---

💧 **Potential for Safe Drinking Water**

Countries with limited freshwater resources can obtain drinking water through **desalination**.

Desalination removes dissolved salts from seawater to produce **potable (safe drinking) water**. This technology is especially important in arid countries where freshwater supplies are scarce.

---

### 📌 Key Takeaways

- Oceans cover **71%** of Earth's surface.
- Continental shelves make up only **10%** of the ocean but provide **95%** of the world's commercial fish catch.
- Oceans provide food, minerals, building materials, renewable energy, transport routes, tourism opportunities, and freshwater.
- Continental shelves are highly productive because they are shallow, well oxygenated, and receive abundant sunlight.
- Desalination converts seawater into potable water for water-scarce regions.
🌊 **World Fisheries**

The distribution of the world's fisheries is closely linked to **ocean currents**, **sunlight**, and the availability of **nutrient-rich waters**. Fish are not evenly distributed throughout the oceans; instead, the richest fishing grounds occur where conditions favour the growth of **phytoplankton**, the foundation of the marine food chain.

---

🌍 **Major Ocean Currents**

Surface ocean currents are mainly driven by **prevailing winds**.

There are two main types:

**Warm currents**
- Originate near the **Equator (Tropics)**.
- Carry warm water towards higher latitudes.

**Cold currents**
- Originate from the **North and South Poles**.
- Carry cold water towards the Equator.

These currents influence climate, nutrient distribution, and the productivity of fisheries.

---

☀️ **The Euphotic Zone**

The **euphotic zone** is the upper layer of the ocean where sunlight can penetrate.

- Extends to approximately **200 metres** deep.
- Provides enough light for **photosynthesis**.
- Supports the growth of phytoplankton.
- Forms the most productive part of the ocean ecosystem.

Most commercial fisheries are found where continental shelves lie within the euphotic zone.

---

🌱 **Marine Food Chain**

Fish populations depend on a healthy marine food chain.

The basic food chain is:

**Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small Fish → Large Fish**

**Phytoplankton**
- Microscopic plant-like organisms.
- Produce food through **photosynthesis**.
- Require:
  - Sunlight
  - Carbon dioxide
  - Water
  - Mineral nutrients

**Zooplankton**
- Tiny animal-like organisms.
- Feed on phytoplankton.
- Form the main food source for many fish species.

Healthy phytoplankton populations support large fish populations and productive fisheries.

---

⬆️ **Upwelling**

Some continental shelves have very few fish despite receiving plenty of sunlight.

The limiting factor is often the lack of **mineral nutrients** such as **nitrogen** and **sulfur**, which phytoplankton require to produce proteins and grow.

The world's richest fishing grounds occur where **upwelling** takes place.

**Upwelling** is the movement of **cold, nutrient-rich water from the deep ocean to the surface**.

Benefits of upwelling:

- Supplies nutrients for phytoplankton growth.
- Increases primary productivity.
- Supports larger populations of zooplankton.
- Provides abundant food for fish.
- Creates some of the world's most productive fisheries.

---

🌡️ **El Niño**

**El Niño** is a natural climatic event that occurs approximately every **4–5 years** in the Pacific Ocean.

During El Niño:

- Warm, nutrient-poor surface water moves eastwards.
- Cold-water upwelling becomes weaker.
- Fewer nutrients reach the ocean surface.
- Phytoplankton populations decline.
- Fish populations decrease significantly.

As a result, commercial fish catches can fall dramatically during El Niño years.

---

### 📌 Key Takeaways

- Surface currents are driven mainly by prevailing winds.
- Warm currents originate near the Equator, while cold currents originate near the poles.
- The **euphotic zone (0–200 m)** receives enough sunlight for photosynthesis.
- **Phytoplankton** form the base of the marine food chain.
- **Upwelling** brings cold, nutrient-rich water to the surface, creating highly productive fisheries.
- **El Niño** weakens upwelling and reduces fish populations.
🐟 **Exploitation of the Oceans: Impact on Fisheries**

Human activities have greatly increased the exploitation of marine resources. Advances in fishing technology and the growing global demand for seafood have placed enormous pressure on fish populations. If harvesting exceeds the natural rate of reproduction, fish stocks begin to decline, making fisheries unsustainable.

---

🎣 **Overfishing**

**Overfishing** occurs when fish are caught **faster than they can reproduce**.

Today, around **75% of the world's marine fisheries are threatened by overfishing**, making sustainable management essential.

### Causes of Overfishing

- Increasing global demand for seafood.
- Population growth.
- Modern fishing technology (GPS, sonar, large fishing vessels).
- Highly efficient fishing nets.
- Lack of effective fisheries management.

### Symptoms of Overfishing

- Reduced fish catches.
- Greater effort needed to catch fish.
- Smaller average size of fish caught.
- Declining fish populations.

Smaller fish are often caught before reaching maturity, preventing them from reproducing and replenishing the population.

---

🐬 **Bycatch**

**Bycatch** refers to the **accidental capture of non-target species** during fishing.

Examples include:

- Dolphins caught while fishing for tuna.
- Sea turtles.
- Sharks.
- Juvenile fish.

Bycatch reduces biodiversity and can threaten endangered species.

---

🕸️ **Fishing Nets and Modern Technology**

Modern technology has made fishing much more efficient, but it has also increased pressure on marine ecosystems.

Common fishing nets include:

- **Trawl nets**
- **Drift nets**
- **Seine nets**
- **Dredge nets**

These large nets can capture huge numbers of fish at once, including unwanted species and juvenile fish, contributing to both overfishing and bycatch.

---

🐠 **Aquaculture and Mariculture**

As wild fish populations decline, fish farming has become increasingly important.

**Aquaculture**
- Farming aquatic organisms in freshwater.
- Usually carried out in fish ponds or tanks.

**Mariculture**
- Farming marine organisms in seawater.
- More difficult and expensive than freshwater aquaculture.
- Practised in countries such as the **USA, Mexico, and Panama**.

### Advantages

- Reduces pressure on wild fish stocks.
- Provides a reliable supply of seafood.
- Can increase food production.

### Disadvantages

- Farmed fish are more vulnerable to diseases and parasites.
- Waste from fish cages can pollute surrounding water.
- Escaped farmed fish may affect wild populations.

---

### 📌 Key Takeaways

- **Overfishing** occurs when fish are harvested faster than they can reproduce.
- Signs of overfishing include smaller catches, greater fishing effort, and smaller fish.
- **Bycatch** is the accidental capture of non-target species.
- Modern fishing technology has increased both overfishing and bycatch.
- **Aquaculture** farms freshwater species, while **mariculture** farms marine species.
- Fish farming helps meet seafood demand but can create pollution and disease problems.
🛡️ **Strategies for Managing the Harvesting of Marine Species**

To ensure fish populations remain sustainable, governments and international organisations use various management strategies. The aim is to allow fish stocks enough time to reproduce while maintaining a reliable supply of seafood.

Every coastal country is responsible for managing fisheries within its **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**.

---

🕸️ **Net Types, Mesh Sizes and Shapes**

Fishing nets should be designed to reduce damage to marine ecosystems.

Management strategies include:

- Banning destructive fishing nets.
- Using **larger mesh sizes** so juvenile fish can escape.
- Catching mainly mature adult fish that have already reproduced.

Recommended mesh sizes include:

- **40 mm square mesh cod end**, or
- **50 mm diamond mesh** for trawling operations.

Using larger mesh sizes helps maintain future fish populations.

---

🎣 **Alternative Fishing Methods**

Some fishing methods are more sustainable than others.

**Fish Aggregating Devices (FADs)**

FADs are floating structures (such as a buoy with a suspended log) that attract fish like tuna.

**Advantages**
- Increase fishing efficiency.
- Reduce the time spent searching for fish.

**Disadvantages**
- Often result in large amounts of **bycatch**, including juvenile fish and dolphins.

**Pole-and-Line Fishing**

Pole-and-line fishing involves catching fish individually using fishing rods from boats.

**Advantages**

- Very little bycatch.
- More selective.
- Causes minimal environmental damage.

Although slower than large-scale net fishing, it is considered one of the most sustainable fishing methods.

---

📋 **Quotas**

A **quota** is the **legal limit on the number or quantity of fish that may be caught**.

Quotas are established by governments or fisheries organisations using scientific data.

Benefits include:

- Prevents overfishing.
- Allows fish populations to reproduce.
- Supports long-term sustainability.
- Protects endangered fish stocks.

---

🚫 **Closed Seasons and Marine Protected Areas**

Governments may temporarily ban fishing during breeding seasons.

**Closed seasons**

- Fishing is prohibited during spawning periods.
- Allows fish to reproduce successfully.

**Marine Protected Areas (MPAs)**

Marine Protected Areas are regions where human activities are restricted to conserve marine ecosystems.

Benefits include:

- Protect breeding grounds.
- Increase biodiversity.
- Help replenish surrounding fish stocks.

---

🌍 **International Agreements and Conservation Laws**

Many fish migrate across international waters, making cooperation between countries essential.

One important agreement is the **United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).**

Key features include:

- Countries manage fisheries within their **200-mile Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**.
- Governments patrol their EEZ to prevent illegal fishing.
- Fishing vessels may be inspected and their catch records checked.
- International agreements regulate fishing in international waters.

These laws help conserve fish populations and protect marine habitats.

---

### 📌 Key Takeaways

- Sustainable fisheries require effective management.
- Larger mesh sizes allow juvenile fish to escape and reproduce.
- **Pole-and-line fishing** is more sustainable than large-scale net fishing.
- **Quotas** limit fish catches to sustainable levels.
- **Closed seasons** protect fish during breeding periods.
- **Marine Protected Areas (MPAs)** conserve important marine habitats.
- **UNCLOS** helps countries manage fisheries within their 200-mile EEZ.

--- 

⚖️ **How Effective Are Fisheries Management Strategies?**

Although many fisheries management strategies exist, enforcing them across the world's oceans remains a major challenge. Oceans cover vast areas, making monitoring difficult and expensive, while illegal fishing continues to threaten marine ecosystems.

### Challenges

- Oceans are extremely large, making surveillance difficult.
- Illegal fishing vessels often operate in poorly patrolled waters.
- Fishermen may underreport catches to avoid quotas.
- Small-mesh nets are sometimes used illegally.
- Fishing may occur inside Marine Protected Areas (MPAs).
- Limited funding and manpower reduce effective enforcement.

---

🚢 **Port-Based Monitoring**

Since continuous ocean patrols are expensive, many countries rely on **port-based monitoring**.

Authorities inspect fishing vessels when they return to port by checking:

- Fishing licences
- Catch records (logbooks)
- Fish species and quantities
- Fishing gear and net types

This is often the most practical and effective way to detect illegal fishing activities.

---

🌍 **Importance of Sustainable Fisheries**

Sustainably managed fisheries are essential because they:

- Provide food for billions of people.
- Support employment in fishing and tourism industries.
- Protect marine biodiversity.
- Maintain healthy marine food webs.
- Ensure fish stocks remain available for future generations.

Without proper management, fish populations may collapse, causing food shortages, economic losses, and serious ecological damage.

---

## 📚 Key Terms

- **Continental Shelf** — The shallow submerged edge of a continent where about **95% of commercial fishing** takes place.
- **Euphotic Zone** — The upper ocean layer (about **200 m deep**) where sunlight allows photosynthesis.
- **Phytoplankton** — Microscopic photosynthetic organisms that form the base of marine food chains.
- **Upwelling** — The movement of cold, nutrient-rich deep water to the surface, increasing fish productivity.
- **El Niño** — A periodic warming of the Pacific Ocean that disrupts upwelling and reduces fish populations.
- **Overfishing** — Catching fish faster than they can naturally reproduce.
- **Bycatch** — Non-target species accidentally caught during fishing.
- **Aquaculture** — Farming freshwater fish and other aquatic organisms.
- **Mariculture** — Farming marine (saltwater) organisms.
- **Quota** — A legal limit on the amount of fish that may be caught.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)** — The area extending **200 nautical miles** from a country's coastline where it has rights to manage marine resources.
- **Marine Protected Area (MPA)** — A protected area where human activities are restricted to conserve marine ecosystems.
- **UNCLOS** — The **United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea**, which regulates the use and conservation of the world's oceans.

---

## 📖 Chapter Summary

Oceans cover **71% of Earth's surface** and provide valuable resources including food, minerals, transport routes, renewable energy, tourism, and freshwater through desalination. Most commercial fishing occurs on **continental shelves**, where sunlight, oxygen, and nutrient-rich waters support abundant marine life.

Marine productivity depends on **phytoplankton**, which require sunlight and nutrients supplied by **upwelling**. Events such as **El Niño** reduce upwelling and can significantly decrease fish populations.

Modern fishing has increased food production but has also caused serious problems such as **overfishing** and **bycatch**. Fish farming through **aquaculture** and **mariculture** helps meet growing demand but may introduce pollution and disease.

Sustainable fisheries are managed using **appropriate mesh sizes, quotas, closed seasons, Marine Protected Areas (MPAs), and international agreements such as UNCLOS**. Although enforcement remains challenging because of illegal fishing and the vast size of the oceans, effective management is essential to conserve marine biodiversity, protect livelihoods, and ensure fish stocks remain available for future generations.
